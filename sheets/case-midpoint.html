The challenge

T-Mobile announced the retirement of the SOAP/XML services supporting critical Inseego DMS workflows, including billing, compliance, inventory, and pricing. The replacement REST APIs were inconsistent, poorly documented, and did not always behave the way the documentation implied.

Five internal application teams were expected to integrate with those services directly. After multiple sprints the migration was stalled — while T-Mobile's external shutdown date remained fixed.

The approach

As part of a small rapid-response team, I worked directly with T-Mobile engineers and business counterparts to identify the missing data, undocumented behavior, and roadmap conflicts that could not be resolved from documentation alone.

I then designed and implemented Midpoint, an integration layer built with AWS API Gateway, Lambda, and TypeScript. Instead of requiring every application team to understand T-Mobile's quirks, Midpoint exposed simpler, stable internal contracts:

  • Centralized validation and data transformation.
  • Partner-specific workarounds and consistent error behavior.
  • Working examples and clearer internal interfaces.
  • One place to absorb future T-Mobile changes.

I also supported the consuming development teams through implementation, training, troubleshooting, and interface negotiation.

The result

All five application teams were unblocked, and the organization completed the migration before T-Mobile's mandatory service sunset.

Midpoint separated volatile partner behavior from Inseego's product teams, and remained important enough that responsibility for it later moved to a dedicated corporate team.

Why it mattered

The critical design decision was not simply to build another API. It was to decide where the complexity should live.

Concentrating unstable partner behavior behind one dependable boundary let five teams build against interfaces they could understand and trust.

Technology

AWS API Gateway · AWS Lambda · TypeScript · REST APIs · Swagger / OpenAPI
