When to Call Me

Most of my work falls into four categories, and all of them tend to have this in common: the technology is entangled with the business, and the entanglement has made things hard.

I’m particularly useful where the problem crosses boundaries: software × operations, greenfield × legacy, business × engineering, internal flows × external vendors.

  • Legacy systems

    An important application needs modernizing—without disrupting the business that depends on it every day.

  • Integrations & vendors

    Multiple systems or outside vendors have to work together, but the interfaces between them are incomplete, undocumented, or unreliable.

  • Operations & workflows

    Operational processes are trapped in spreadsheets, paper, disparate tools, and nobody seems to have the whole picture.

  • Stalled initiatives

    Between business requirements and technical execution, a project is stuck, and the impasse is not going away on its own.

How I Work

Discover → Decompose → Build & integrate → Production

Discovery, training, stakeholder alignment, change management are integral to technical delivery, not afterthoughts.

  1. Discover

    Learn how the business actually works; interview the people doing the work; read real paperwork, spreadsheets, and reports: watch the workflow rather than assume the needs.

  2. Decompose

    Find the boundaries and patterns that make re-engineering feasible; decide what is worth preserving, where problems should be contained, whether complexity is essential.

  3. Build & integrate

    Implement what is missing and integrate what should be retained. I write production code myself; the discovery and architecting is realized by a hands-on delivery.

  4. Production

    Continuously involved through migration, rollout, training, adoption, and beyond. A problem is not solved when code merges, but when stakeholders are happy.
